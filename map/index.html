What Curiosity measured

Curiosity scooped sand near small ripple crests. CheMin used X-ray diffraction on grains smaller than 150 µm. The four largest mineral shares below describe the crystalline portion of that sample.

CheMin · crystalline mass fraction · ±1σ
Plagioclase36.5 ± 0.8%
Olivine25.8 ± 0.4%
Augite22.0 ± 0.4%
Pigeonite10.6 ± 0.4%

CheMin also estimated that 35 ± 15% of the whole sample was X-ray amorphous. CheMin analysis, Table 1

Compare the size fractions

APXS measured 7.57 wt% magnesium oxide in the fine discard pile A (<150 µm) and 11.50 wt% in coarse pile B (>150 µm). The higher value in the coarse pile shows how grain size affects the chemistry measured at one site. Campaign synthesis, Table 3

How the dunes hold heat

Mars Odyssey’s THEMIS instrument gave a field-wide apparent thermal inertia of 240 ± 20 J m⁻² K⁻¹ s⁻½. Models using Curiosity ground temperatures at Namib gave 170–200 in the same units. The observations cover different areas and help explain how sand heats and cools. THEMIS analysis · Namib comparison

Curiosity DAN · Namib traverse

DAN uses neutrons to estimate hydrogen in the upper ~60 cm. Water-equivalent hydrogen (WEH) expresses that hydrogen as a water mass fraction. The signal can include hydrogen bound in minerals.

Gobabeb-area sand · active sample
Records within the Namib map
Circles show active samples. Squares show passive samples.

DAN records follow the rover’s path. Unmarked areas have no mapped estimate in this dataset. Deeper layers are beyond DAN’s sensing depth.

Across Curiosity’s broader Gale traverse through sol 3333, DAN estimates ranged from near zero to 6.1 ± 0.7 wt% WEH. At Gobabeb, heating sand in the rover’s SAM instrument released water at temperatures consistent with hydrogen bound in minerals.

ISRU planning

Recoverable water at Namib remains unverified. Estimating a supply requires samples that identify the form of the water, the deposit’s depth and extent, and a test that measures extraction yield, energy use, and water quality.

What InSight tells us about Gale

InSight measured shaking at one station in Elysium Planitia. Many located events cluster near Cerberus Fossae, east of the lander. Many other records give a distance without a direction. The catalog leaves Gale’s local shaking and quake frequency unresolved.

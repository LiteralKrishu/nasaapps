# Marswalk research and data audit

Reviewed 14 November 2026. Scope includes the README, all six research documents present before this audit, the challenge brief, the five supplied resources, and the primary data sources below.

The [Namib Dune prototype](../map/index.html) now includes the aligned elevation and image crops, the historical rover track, and a calculated detour. The [prototype notes](../map/README.md) record the checks and the remaining coverage limits. The findings below capture the readiness assessment before the map build.

## Readiness decision

**The research is sufficient to start one site-specific prototype.** Public records support a multi-mission Marswalk at Bagnold Dunes on lower Mount Sharp. Before this review, the repository contained research notes and source links. It had no downloaded source data, alignment results, calculated route, or map implementation. This audit adds a weather sample and identifies the products to use first. File-level raster coverage and the route still need verification.

The challenge asks for an integrated view of one location or route that uses data from multiple NASA science missions and supports both navigation and science. The brief suggests at least three **types** of data and allows static maps, interactive tools, historical conditions, and modeled conditions. The 19 topics below are the project’s research checklist. The official Resources tab returned an access error during review. The text and its five resource links were cross-checked against the HackAthlone event copy and the individual providers. [NASA challenge Resources tab](https://www.spaceappschallenge.org/2026/challenges/interplanetary-survival-guide-martian-map/?tab=resources) · [HackAthlone challenge copy](https://hackathlone.com/challenges/interplanetary-survival-guide-martian-map)

## Recommended demonstration

Use the **High Dune to Namib Dune area in Gale Crater**. Curiosity drove between the dunes during mission sols 1185–1196, then sampled sand at Gobabeb on sol 1225. The science task is to compare sand ripples and determine how wind sorts the dune material. Use the rover track as a historical reference. Plot a separate simulated out-and-back Marswalk from a base marker to an observation point beside the dune, with a stop to inspect the Gobabeb results. Center the initial map near **4.700°S, 137.3583°E**, the center of a published Bagnold overview map. Obtain exact waypoint coordinates from the rover localization data and campaign map. [Campaign overview](https://doi.org/10.1029/2018GL079032) · [Sample record](https://doi.org/10.1002/2017JE005262) · [Published map center](https://doi.org/10.1029/2018GL079029)

Start with optical, topographic, thermal, and mineralogical data from three NASA missions. Use MRO imagery and elevation, Mars Odyssey THEMIS thermal inertia, and Curiosity science observations. Add historical weather once the selected REMS files are checked. The same site offers a documented dune hazard, a science stop with measured mineralogy, and surface photographs that show what the crew would see. [USGS Gale terrain](https://astrogeology.usgs.gov/search/map/mars_msl_gale_merged_dem_1m) · [Bagnold thermophysical study](https://doi.org/10.1029/2017JE005501) · [Curiosity view at Namib Dune](https://www.jpl.nasa.gov/images/pia20755-two-sizes-of-ripples-on-surface-of-martian-sand-dune/)

## Nineteen-topic coverage check

**Use** means an identified product or published result is sufficient for the stated demo role once brought into the map. **Derive** means the prototype still needs a calculation or an explicit model. **Context** means the source supports a reference panel or replay.

| Topic | Readiness and evidence |
| --- | --- |
| Atmospheric & meteorological data | **Use.** Curiosity REMS provides Gale pressure, air and ground temperature, humidity, and UV observations. Select the historical campaign window and keep the rover position and time with each reading. MEDA observations apply to Jezero. [REMS archive](https://pds-atmospheres.nmsu.edu/data_and_services/atmospheres_data/MARS/curiosity/rems.html) |
| Topographic & geophysical data | **Use for terrain.** USGS lists a MOLA-tied 1 m Gale elevation mosaic. MOLA supplies regional relief. InSight interior models describe planetary structure. [Gale DEM](https://astrogeology.usgs.gov/search/map/mars_msl_gale_merged_dem_1m) · [MOLA product](https://pds.nasa.gov/ds-view/pds/viewProfile.jsp?dsid=MGS-M-MOLA-5-MEGDR-L3-V1.0) · [InSight interior analysis](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-023-06586-4) |
| Elevation & contour mapping | **Derive.** Crop the Gale DEM to the route area. Derive contours, slope, and the route elevation profile from a metric Mars grid. Carry the elevation reference, source footprints, and NoData mask into each output. [Gale DEM](https://astrogeology.usgs.gov/search/map/mars_msl_gale_merged_dem_1m) · [USGS guidance on stereo DEM artifacts](https://stac.astrogeology.usgs.gov/docs/data/mars/hirise_dtms/) |
| Geological & chemical data | **Use.** Gobabeb has CheMin mineral abundances and APXS composition. Show these at the sampling site. Use USGS SIM 3292 for regional geologic context and the local campaign mapping for dune boundaries. [CheMin and APXS tables](https://doi.org/10.1002/2017JE005262) · [USGS geologic map](https://pubs.usgs.gov/publication/sim3292) · [Dune campaign map](https://science.nasa.gov/resource/curiositys-bagnold-dunes-campaign-two-types-of-dunes/) |
| Radiation & space weather data | **Context.** Curiosity RAD archives surface measurements. MAVEN SEP measured particles in Mars orbit. NASA declared MAVEN’s mission ended on 3 June 2026. Pair the two records for a dated event. [RAD archive](https://pds-ppi.igpp.ucla.edu/mission/Mars_Science_Laboratory) · [MAVEN SEP archive](https://pds.nasa.gov/ds-view/pds/viewBundle.jsp?identifier=urn%3Anasa%3Apds%3Amaven.sep.calibrated&version=2.26) · [NASA mission status](https://science.nasa.gov/mission/maven/) |
| Ionizing & solar radiation on Mars | **Context.** Show the early Gale GCR-dominated baseline and the May 2024 solar event as distinct observations. Keep absorbed dose in Gy and dose equivalent in Sv, with the detector and integration period. UV is a separate field. [RAD early record](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1244797) · [May 2024 event](https://www.jpl.nasa.gov/news/nasa-watches-mars-light-up-during-epic-solar-storm/) |
| Hydrological & resource data | **Context.** SWIM provides regional ice-consistency maps at about 3 km/pixel, with depth classes of 0–1 m, 1–5 m, and deeper than 5 m. The reviewed sources establish no candidate extractable ice on the demo route. Jezero RIMFAX shows local subsurface structure there. [SWIM GIS products](https://swim.psi.edu/) · [SWIM methodology](https://www.usgs.gov/publications/refined-mapping-subsurface-water-ice-mars-support-future-missions) · [Jezero RIMFAX results](https://doi.org/10.3847/PSJ/ad6445) |
| In-situ resource utilization (ISRU) potential | **Context.** MOXIE produced 122 g of oxygen across 16 runs at Jezero. It peaked at 12 g/h at 98% purity or better. A local resource model would need verified feedstock, power, throughput, and storage assumptions. [JPL MOXIE results](https://www.jpl.nasa.gov/news/nasas-oxygen-generating-experiment-moxie-completes-mars-mission/) |
| Navigation & spatial data (multi-layered mapping) | **Derive.** NASA’s PLACES localization files give a rover route reference. Register the raster layers and samples to the same Mars coordinates. Check their alignment at known points before measuring distances. [PLACES data](https://planetarydata.jpl.nasa.gov/img/data/msl/msl_places/data_localizations/) · [Localization specification](https://planetarydata.jpl.nasa.gov/w10n/msl/msl_places/document/PLACES_PDS_SIS.pdf) |
| Optical & spectral imagery | **Use.** HiRISE image `ESP_035917_1755` is the background of NASA’s Bagnold campaign map. Curiosity Mastcam gives the Namib ground view. Published CRISM results distinguish dune materials at selected locations. Use footprints and acquisition dates for every image. [HiRISE observation](https://www.uahirise.org/ESP_035917_1755) · [Mastcam panorama](https://www.jpl.nasa.gov/images/pia20755-two-sizes-of-ripples-on-surface-of-martian-sand-dune/) · [CRISM dune study](https://doi.org/10.1002/2016JE005133) |
| Route planning & hazard avoidance | **Derive.** The Bagnold campaign documents mobile sand and a slip face. Trace a dated dune boundary, review DEM slopes, and compare a direct line with a detour. Terrain outside good coverage stays unknown. Rover history gives a reference for the route review. [Campaign overview](https://doi.org/10.1029/2018GL079032) · [Namib slip face](https://science.nasa.gov/resource/slip-face-on-downwind-side-of-namib-sand-dune-on-mars-2/) |
| Closed-loop life support metrics | **Context and simulation.** NASA’s baseline values support documented inputs. ISS hardware demonstrated 98% water recovery with brine processing. A suit budget needs separate assumptions for oxygen, carbon dioxide removal, battery, and cooling water. Use the limiting resource and reserve in the simulated return check. [NASA baseline values](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/20210024855) · [ISS water recovery test](https://www.nasa.gov/missions/station/nasa-achieves-water-recovery-milestone-on-international-space-station/) |
| Gravitational adaptation | **Context.** Surface gravity is about 3.71 m/s², or 38% of Earth’s. Sustained human adaptation at Mars gravity remains uncertain. Use walking speed and fatigue as simulation inputs with explicit assumptions. [Mars planetary facts](https://www.jpl.nasa.gov/news/press_kits/insight/landing/facts/mars-at-a-glance/) · [NASA partial-gravity research](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/20205010170) |
| Psychological & temporal data | **Use for time. Context for psychology.** A mean solar day lasts 24 h 39 min 35.244 s. Preserve UTC, mission sol and the time convention for each measurement. Mars500 tracked six people during a 520-day Earth-based confinement study. Use it as isolation and sleep context. [NASA Mars24 worked examples](https://www.giss.nasa.gov/tools/mars24/help/algorithm.html) · [Mars24 technical notes](https://www.giss.nasa.gov/tools/mars24/help/notes.html) · [Mars500 study](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1212646110) |
| Space weather alerts | **Context and replay.** The May 2024 storm is a documented Mars event with reusable research data. NOAA GOES alerts use near-Earth measurements. NASA SEPSTER3D models Mars proton conditions with uncertainty. A fixed alert lead time is unsupported. [May 2024 event](https://www.jpl.nasa.gov/news/nasa-watches-mars-light-up-during-epic-solar-storm/) · [Replay data](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.14801849) · [NOAA event definitions](https://www.swpc.noaa.gov/sites/default/files/images/u97/Space%20Weather%20101.pptx_.pdf) · [NASA Mars SEP model](https://ccmc.gsfc.nasa.gov/models/SEPSTER3D~0.1/) |
| Seismic data | **Context.** The InSight Marsquake Service V14 catalog gives historical events from a single station in Elysium Planitia. Use catalog location uncertainty for regional context. Gale-specific shaking and subsurface stability require local evidence. [Marsquake Service V14](https://www.insight.ethz.ch/en/seismicity/catalog/v14/) |
| Diurnal/seasonal temperature swings | **Use.** Plot REMS air and ground temperature separately by local Mars time for a campaign sol. Compare the same local time across seasons using solar longitude (Ls), while preserving measurement locations and coverage. [REMS archive](https://pds-atmospheres.nmsu.edu/data_and_services/atmospheres_data/MARS/curiosity/rems.html) · [Gale energy budget across 2,500 sols](https://doi.org/10.1029/2020JE006804) |
| Weather & wind patterns | **Use with gaps.** The Bagnold study reports measured winds and separate MarsWRF simulations. REMS wind coverage is partial after sensor damage. MRO’s Mars Climate Sounder and the Mars Climate Database provide regional dust or modeled conditions. [Bagnold wind study](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/20180000151) · [REMS wind limitations](https://pds-atmospheres.nmsu.edu/data_and_services/atmospheres_data/MARS/curiosity/rems_wind.html) · [MRO dust archive](https://pds-atmospheres.nmsu.edu/data_and_services/atmospheres_data/MARS/aerosols.html) · [Mars Climate Database v6.2](https://www-mars.lmd.jussieu.fr/mcd_python/) |
| Gas compositions | **Use as a Gale site reference.** Curiosity SAM found annual mean volume fractions of 95.1% CO₂, 2.59% N₂, 1.94% ⁴⁰Ar, 0.161% O₂, and 0.058% CO. These are Gale measurements across multiple seasonal cycles. Use REMS pressure at the intended sol and time for any partial-pressure calculation. [SAM seasonal composition and measurement table](https://doi.org/10.1029/2019JE006175) |

### Values that can go into the demo now

The [Gale REMS sample CSV](data/gale-rems-sam-weather-samples.csv) contains 12 selected observations from Trainer et al., Table 1. It retains fractional Curiosity mission sol, Mars year, solar longitude, local mean solar time (LMST), air and ground temperature, pressure, reported uncertainties, and SAM test ID. Pressure and its uncertainty are converted from mbar to Pa at 100 Pa per mbar. These are individual measurements taken during atmospheric sampling elsewhere on the Gale traverse, including sols before and after the selected dune interval. Use them to test the weather display and illustrate the range of observations. Use the campaign archive for weather at the demo route. [Trainer et al., Table 1](https://doi.org/10.1029/2019JE006175)

| Observation | Value and scope |
| --- | --- |
| Gobabeb crystalline mineralogy | The <150 µm Gobabeb sample’s crystalline fraction contained 36.5 wt% plagioclase, 25.8 wt% olivine, 22.0 wt% augite, and 10.6 wt% pigeonite. These percentages describe the crystalline fraction of one sample. [CheMin Table 1](https://doi.org/10.1002/2017JE005262) |
| Apparent thermal inertia | Curiosity ground-temperature models at Namib gave 170–200 J m⁻² K⁻¹ s⁻½ for sols 1222–1242. An orbital THEMIS estimate for the Bagnold dunes was 240 ± 20 in the same units, with a different albedo assumption and footprint. These estimates describe thermal response. The route needs independent evidence for terrain mobility. [Thermal comparison](https://doi.org/10.1029/2021JE007076) |
| Bagnold winds | At the lee of Namib, the dune blocked northerly winds and measurements showed a primarily westerly daytime component during the observation period. Wind behavior depends on the side of the dune and time of day. [REMS and MarsWRF analysis](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/20180000151) |
| Surface radiation | Early Curiosity data yielded about 0.64 ± 0.12 mSv/day dose equivalent in a GCR-dominated interval. On **20 May 2024 (Curiosity sol 4190)**, JPL reported 8,100 µGy absorbed dose for the solar event. Use this as a separate replay from the Bagnold campaign. [Hassler et al.](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1244797) · [JPL event dose](https://www.jpl.nasa.gov/news/nasa-watches-mars-light-up-during-epic-solar-storm/) · [NASA event sol](https://science.nasa.gov/resource/curiosity-sees-streaks-and-specks-during-2024-solar-storm/) |

## Checks before using the data in the prototype

1. Crop the imagery and numerical elevation to the same corridor. Read each source’s body, latitude convention, longitude direction and domain, projection, elevation datum, grid spacing, and acquisition date. Use Mars coordinates and a metric Mars projection for the slope, area, and distance calculations. Compare common landmarks and rover fixes on the map.
2. Derive slope, hillshade, contours, and the elevation profile. Verify the elevation and distance at known points and inspect seams along the proposed path. Keep NoData and low-resolution fill visible as uncertainty. Use THEMIS as material context. Trace known hazards separately from dated orbital and rover images.
3. Add the position and source of each science stop. Compare the direct line with the proposed detour and show distance, ascent, slope, reason for the detour, and return margin. Record any slope limits, walking speeds, and suit reserves as adjustable simulation assumptions. The project notes previously used both 20% and 25% return margins.
4. Keep terrain acquisition dates, rover mission sols, and event timestamps visible. Attach the archive product and units to each observation. Show rover weather and radiation as point or time-series records. Label modeled conditions, and display missing values as unknown. Treat the 2024 solar storm as its own timeline event.

Catalog metadata, published results, and the archive indexes were reviewable. The workspace could not fetch the source files directly during the audit. The Gale product bounds overlap the map center, and sample values above come from published measurements. A downloaded crop, profile, and calculated detour are still needed to verify the local map.
